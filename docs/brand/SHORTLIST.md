# Mark shortlist (2026-11-01)

Source: eight imagegen directions in `concepts/` (Codex gpt-6.1-sol, `concepts/CONCEPTS.md`), judged by eye against the `logo-design` application-context test: 16 px template glyph in the menu bar, 28 px, 1024 px Dock icon beside Raycast, Cursor, Arc and Linear, favicon in a tab strip, single colour, reverse.

Verdict on the eight: they are concept studies, not marks. Runner reads as plumbing, Crucible as a mug, Command Joint as a chain link, Strike as a download arrow, Split Mould as a pair of code brackets. Three survive as seeds. A fourth seed comes from the site itself.

## 1. Pressed tile (recommended)

Not in the Codex set. A 2 × 2 of chalk keycap tiles on the site's grid; one tile is pressed, so its orange side shows and its face sits lower. The site's WebGL tiles already have chalk faces and orange sides (`shelf-scene.ts`, `side = #f36f39`), so the mark is the existing material doing one honest thing: one key, pressed.

- Says: one keystroke, everything. The pressed tile is the full stop the headlines already end with.
- 16 px: a 2 × 2 with one cell different. Four squares survive any reduction; the pressed cell is darker in a template glyph (no colour needed).
- App icon: the 2 × 2 fills the squircle; the pressed tile throws a short shadow; nothing else.
- Keeps: chalk tiles, orange as the one active thing, Geist, the full stop rule.
- Risk: resembles a Windows or a dashboard glyph if the tiles are equal. The press (lower face, visible side) must be unmistakable, and the grid gap must match the wordmark's.

## 2. Registration (Codex 06)

Four corner pieces around one clear space, one of them orange. Strongest continuation of the tile language, and the orange "fourth part" is the same idea as the full stop.

- 16 px: reads as a scan, focus or fullscreen glyph. macOS uses this shape in Camera, QR scanning and Spotlight's image search. The asymmetry has to be heavy for it to be ours.
- Keeps: tiles, orange full stop, Geist.
- Risk: the most familiar silhouette in the set.

## 3. Spacebar (Codex 08)

Two end keys and a depressed centre bar. The most literal ⌘ Space mark.

- 16 px: a battery or a dumbbell. The press depth does not survive the reduction.
- Keeps: keycaps, orange as the active key, Geist.
- Risk: literal, and the wide aspect fights a square icon.

## 4. Ingot Gate (Codex 03)

A solid trapezoid with one cutout. Cleanest single contour in the set.

- 16 px: an arch or a tunnel. Anonymous without the orange.
- Keeps: the orange full stop, Geist. Loses the tiles entirely.
- Risk: it belongs to any company.

## Decision (user, 2026-11-01)

Split Mould (Codex 02) goes to production, Command Joint (Codex 05) is the alternate. The user's words: "i like command joint and split mould. i think split mold might win though." The agent's recommendation below is kept for the record and is not the decision.

What Split Mould has to prove in Task A3 before it is final: at 16 px it must not read as a pair of code brackets `[ ]` or as a QR glyph; the channel between the halves must stay at least 2 device pixels at 1x; the cast in the centre is the only orange element. If the first vector fails the bracket reading, draw Command Joint next, with the loops kept to two so it never becomes the ⌘ symbol.

## Agent recommendation (superseded)

Pressed tile. It is built from the material the website already owns, it survives 16 px as four squares with one difference, and it means exactly what the product does. Registration is the fallback if the pressed tile reads as a window grid after a first vector draw.

Production of the chosen mark is Task A3 in `docs/plans/2026-11-01-foundry-last-20.md`: vector redraw on the tile grid, Dock and menu bar and tab-strip captures beside the neighbours, and the mark is only done when it does not look weaker than them.
